Martin Luther King, JR.

  • Biography
  • Timeline

Martin Luther King Jr was an American civil rights activist and Baptist minister who is widely regarded as America's pre-eminent advocate of novionlence, and one of the greatest nonviolent leaders in world history. Among the many awards and honors he recieved, King was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1964, the same year President Johnson signed the Civil Rights Act into law. Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr's nonviolent leadership ended abruptly and tragically on April 4th, 1968, when he was assasinated at the Lorraine Motel in Memphis, Tennessee.

Dr. King drew inspiration from both his faith in Christianity and the peaceful teachings of Mahatma Ghandi, when he lead his nonviolent movement. What Martin Luther King, Jr. wanted to achieve through the movement, was legal equality for African-Americans in the united states. Unlike the others who were advacating for freedom by "any means necessary," including violence, Dr. King used the power of words and acts of nonviolent resistance, such as protests, grassroots organizations, and civil disobediene to achieve goals that were seemingly-impossible.

Dr. King's "I have a Dream" speech, Nobel Peace Prize lecture and "Letter from a birmingham Jail" are among the most revered orations and wwritings in the English language. All of his accomplishments have been taught to children of all races since his death, and his teachings are studied by scholars and students worldwide.
