About the artist

I’m Elena Marlow, a painter and mixed-media artist based in the Pacific Northwest. I’ve been creating art for over 15 years, ever since I first picked up a charcoal stick in a high school life-drawing class. What began as a hobby quickly grew into a lifelong dedication to visual storytelling.

My work is inspired by natural decay, memory, and quiet landscapes. I often find myself drawn to the textures of old walls, the softness of morning fog, and the way light filters through forest canopies. Artists like Andrew Wyeth, Anselm Kiefer, and my grandmother (a quilt maker) have deeply shaped how I see surface and story.

My goals as an artist

  • Create honest, tactile work that invites viewers to slow down and notice details.
  • Build a sustainable studio practice that balances gallery shows, commissions, and direct sales to collectors.
  • Mentor emerging artists through local workshops and open-studio events.
  • Expand my series “Quiet Structures” into a full exhibition by 2027.
“I don’t try to capture a place exactly — I try to capture the feeling of standing there, half-remembered, with the light shifting.”

Process · how each work is made

Every piece begins with observation and sketch. Below is a general overview of the tools and methods I use across different media.

Tools & methods by medium
Stage Tools / materials Method
Drawing & prep Graphite, charcoal, kneaded eraser, sketchbook Thumbnail sketches, tonal studies, and value maps before committing to a final surface.
Oil painting Linen or birch panel, hog bristle brushes, palette knife, walnut oil Layered indirect painting — thin washes, then thicker impasto. Often finished with a matte varnish.
Mixed media Acrylic matte medium, rice paper, wax pastels, sandpaper Collage and scraping. I build up textures, then sand back to reveal earlier layers.
Encaustic Beeswax, damar resin, heated palette, Hake brushes Molten wax is fused with a heat gun. Each layer is fused before the next is added.
Finishing Soft cloth, archival varnish, frame samples Works are signed, dated, and photographed. Framing options discussed with the collector.

For commissions, I usually share WIP photos and notes at key stages so you can see the piece evolve.
